# STS2 Strategy Guide

This is the durable strategy guide for lessons supported by repeated runs or high-confidence observations. Keep exploratory balance claims in `playtests/runs/` until they have enough evidence to generalize.

## Evidence Standard

- State the character, mechanic, card, or encounter to which a lesson applies.
- Prefer observations reproduced across multiple combats or runs.
- Distinguish strategic advice from suspected balance problems and game bugs.
- Include important conditions, dependencies, and known failure modes.
- Replace outdated guidance when a game or character update changes behavior.

## Character Strategies

### Cyclops (Cyclops mod)

**Core mechanic — Breach (confirmed intended, not a bug):** Breach damage is *unblockable* damage, and Breach cards (Optic Breach, Precision Shot, Breach 5, and Strikes/other cards granted Breach by Phoenix Force Visor) always count as "breaking" the target's Block for the purposes of Phoenix Force Visor and Break Their Guard — **even when the target currently has 0 Block.** There is no requirement that the enemy have positive Block for the break-triggered effects (Vulnerable, temporary Strength, energy refund via Break Their Guard, card draw) to fire. This has now reproduced across two full playtests (E05/E08/E11/E17 in `modded:profile1:1787866435`, and the prior run) and is treated as the character's baseline engine, not an anomaly. Source-confirmed: `research/cyclops-card-core-mechanic-audit.md` in `sts2-Cyclops` classifies Breach as a native "Unblockable" damage primitive with a custom Block-broken dispatch layered on top — the dispatch does not gate on the target's current Block value.

Practical implications for the playtest agent:
- Do not treat a zero-Block trigger as surprising, ambiguous, or evidence of a bug. Record it as expected Breach behavior unless the *specific* interaction (e.g., a listener firing for the wrong relic type, or double-triggering) looks structurally wrong.
- Because Breach reliably produces Vulnerable + temporary Strength + (with Break Their Guard) energy/draw regardless of enemy Block, a hand with 2-3 Breach-capable attacks is usually already "built" for the core engine. Drafting an additional redundant Breach attack (e.g., a third Optic Breach) when the deck already has multiple breaching Strikes/attacks is low marginal value — prefer defense, card draw, or a card that addresses the setup-tempo gap (see below) instead.
- Optic Breach/Precision Shot/Breach 5 do not need a "positive Block" target to be worth playing — do not undervalue them against enemies at 0 Block, and do not delay playing them waiting for a "better" target.

**Setup-tempo bias — read before evaluating Powers:** Cyclops's persistent Powers (Field Commander, Countermeasures, Break Their Guard) look attractive in isolation because they are a one-time energy/card cost for an ongoing per-turn benefit. That framing is true but incomplete: the ongoing benefit only pays off in future turns, while the setup cost is paid immediately, in tempo, against a clock (incoming damage) that does not pause. Two playtests have now shown the same failure shape: multiple setup Powers get installed early because each one is individually justifiable, but the deck ends up in an "expensive setup zone" — several energy/cards spent on engine pieces before compact, immediate Block exists — and takes a large HP hit (bygone Effigy: 50 HP; the Act 2 boss: full loss) before the engine's payoff outweighs its up-front cost.

When evaluating a Power pick or play, weigh explicitly:
- **Immediate cost**: energy this turn, a card this turn/next reward slot, and — critically — the Block or damage you are *not* doing this turn because you spent the turn setting up instead.
- **Payoff horizon**: how many turns until the Power's ongoing benefit (Tactical Mastery generation, Thorns, Breach-to-energy conversion) has repaid its setup cost in Block/damage-equivalent terms.
- **Current HP and Block density**: do not stack a second or third setup Power in the same combat/early floors if the deck does not yet have enough immediate Block (Shield 5 tokens, Evasive Maneuvers, Defends) in hand or expected within the next 1-2 draws to survive an average hit in the meantime. Prefer a defensive card or a single setup Power over compounding multiple setup Powers before turn 3-4 of a fight, especially at less than ~70% HP.
- Do not equate "Powers are individually strong" with "installing all available Powers early is correct." Record explicitly, at each Power pick/play decision, what near-term Block or damage was forgone and whether the HP total at that point could absorb an average hit while unprotected.

**Powers reset between combats (not persistent across fights):** Confirmed directly in `modded:profile1:1787950058` (Bowlbug fight, Act 2 Floor 20): Good Guy Protocol, Break Their Guard, and Definitely the Good Guy all had to be re-played from the hand at the start of a new combat — observed as ordinary unplayed cards sitting in the draw pile at the fight's start, not as already-active statuses carried over from the previous fight. Do not assume a Power played in a prior combat is still providing its ongoing effect in a new fight; always check whether it needs to be replayed. High confidence (direct, unambiguous before/after state observation).

**Enemy "Imbalanced" Debuff — full-block triggers a Stun (exploitable stun-lock):** Some enemies (confirmed on Bowlbug/Rock, Act 2) carry an "Imbalanced" Debuff: if their telegraphed attack is fully blocked (player Block >= the attack's damage value at the moment it resolves), the enemy becomes Stunned and skips its next turn. This creates a repeatable loop for a Block-capable deck: fully block the telegraphed attack -> enemy is Stunned -> take a free all-offense turn -> enemy's attack telegraphs again -> fully block again. Reproduced twice in one fight (`modded:profile1:1787950058`, E23). When facing an Imbalanced enemy, prioritize matching or exceeding its exact telegraphed attack value with Block over merely reducing incoming damage, since a full block is disproportionately more valuable than a partial one against this Debuff. Medium-high confidence (2 clean reproductions, single enemy type so far).

**Enemy "Tender" Debuff — a direct counter to Block-stacking turns:** Some enemies (confirmed on Hunter Killer, Act 2) can apply a "Tender" Debuff: whenever the player plays a card, they lose 1 Strength and 1 Dexterity for the rest of that turn. Because Dexterity scales Block gained from cards, this decays the value of every subsequent card played in the same turn — directly punishing the common Cyclops pattern of stacking several cheap Block/Skill cards (Defend, We've Trained for This, Wide Angle Blast+) in one turn rather than committing to 1-2 high-value Block cards. Confirmed in `modded:profile1:1787950058` (E26/E26a): Block values from successive cards in the same turn measurably declined as more cards were played, and this was the proximate mechanism of that run's death (a Block-less hand met a lethal attack with no other mitigation available). Practical implication: against a Tender-carrying enemy, prefer playing your single highest-Block card first (before any Dex loss) and consider whether a big single Block card (Equilibrium, We've Trained for This) preserves more total Block than several small ones. Medium confidence pending a second Tender-carrying enemy encounter to confirm this is not encounter-specific.

**Debuff-driven intent changes recalculate live, not just at resolution:** Applying a damage-reducing debuff to an enemy (Weak, or a Strength-loss effect like Off My Lawn) immediately updates that enemy's displayed attack-intent value in the very same state read, before the attack actually resolves — confirmed across 3 separate instances in two different runs (a Strength-crash on Vantom in `modded:profile1:1787950058`'s Act 1 boss fight, and two separate Weak applications on Hunter Killer in the same run's Act 2 death fight). This means intent labels can be trusted as live/current rather than "as originally telegraphed," which is useful for deciding whether a debuff play has already made an attack survivable before committing further Block. High confidence (3 reproductions, 2 debuff types, 2 enemies).

**"Bound" keyword and "Chains of Binding" status (Act 3 dual-boss encounter, Torch Head Amalgam + Queen):** Some late-game cards/enemies interact with a "Bound" affliction: "Only 1 Bound card can be played each turn. Cards are un-Bound at end of turn." Confirmed in `modded:profile1:1788029746` that the source of Bound in this specific fight is a persistent player-side status power, **Chains of Binding** ("The first 3 cards drawn each turn are Afflicted with Bound"), visible in the player's status list — not a per-card enemy debuff applied at cast time. This meaningfully constrains turn planning: multiple useful Bound cards (Block skills, AoE attacks, etc.) may be drawn in the same hand, but only one can be played, so pick the single highest-value Bound card for the situation (e.g., prioritize Block density over damage when facing a large incoming hit) and treat the rest as dead weight for that turn. The precise trigger that installs Chains of Binding on the player (Queen's "Malicious"/CardDebuff intent is the leading candidate) was not conclusively pinned down in this run — flagged as an open question for a future encounter with the same boss. Medium-high confidence (mechanic behavior directly observed via card `can_play`/`unplayable_reason: BlockedByHook` state and the status list; trigger source unconfirmed).

**Ricochet Beam and multi-hit Breach cards proc on-break relics per-hit, not per-card or per-unique-target:** Confirmed in `playtests/runs/cyclops-2026-08-30-a.md` (E18/E19/E21) across 3 combats with different enemy counts (3-enemy, 4-enemy, and 1-enemy fields): Ricochet Beam's "Deal 3 Breach damage 4 times to random enemies" independently triggers Cyclops Battle Visor (and other on-break effects like Break Their Guard/Crippling Breach) once per individual hit. In multi-enemy fights this can land multiple hits on the same target, stacking Vulnerable further on that target (observed stacking to amount 2 from 2 hits in a 3-enemy fight). In single-enemy fights, all 4 hits collapse onto the sole target, making Ricochet Beam a guaranteed quadruple-proc single-target nuke (16+ raw Breach damage plus up to 4 Vulnerable/temp-Strength stacks for 2 energy) — strictly stronger in that specific case than its "random multi-target" design might suggest. Medium-high confidence (3 reproductions across varied enemy counts, consistent behavior each time); the balance implication (whether this single-target collapse is intended or overtuned) remains open — see the source run report's Next Experiments for follow-up.

**Known card values (update as balance changes land):**
- Plan 2 → Shield 5 mode: converts a hand card into a token that grants 5 Block **and 5 Tactical Mastery** (updated 2026-08-28; previously Block-only). This makes the defensive mode also a light offensive setup (pairs with Find the Opening / other Tactical Mastery consumers) rather than a pure Block sink.
- Evasive Maneuvers: 0-energy card granting 6 Block (updated 2026-08-28, up from 4) specifically to give it a defensible floor without conflicting with Orichalcum's 6-Block-on-empty-turn trigger — a lone Evasive Maneuvers now matches Orichalcum's threshold instead of undercutting it.
- Track whether the Shield 5 + Tactical Mastery change measurably improves Plan 2's usefulness in Find the Opening / offensive lines, and whether Evasive Maneuvers' new value removes the previously observed tension with Orichalcum, in the next 1-2 runs before promoting further conclusions.

### Silent (base game)

**Innate Powers avoid the setup-tempo tax documented for Cyclops:** Infinite Blades+ (Innate, adds 1 Shiv/turn) reliably appeared in the opening hand of every combat it was drafted for (5/5 combats in `playtests/runs/silent-2026-08-31.md`) at zero draw cost, and its 1-energy cast is a one-time-per-combat cost rather than a recurring card-slot tax. This means the Cyclops-derived caution against drafting multiple setup Powers early (see Cyclops section above) applies less strongly to Innate Powers specifically — an Innate Power's "setup cost" is paid once per combat rather than compounding with card-draw variance. Still weigh the 1-energy cast against that turn's alternative use, but do not treat Innate as automatically equivalent to non-Innate setup-tempo risk. Medium confidence (single run, single Power, 5 combats).

**Enemy "Debuff"/"Strategic" intents are not reliably self-directed:** Confirmed twice in one run (Sludge Spinner in `playtests/runs/silent-2026-08-31.md` E05, and Gremlin Merc in the same report's E10) that an enemy's Debuff-labeled intent can apply Weak to the **player**, not to the enemy itself. Do not assume a "Debuff" intent is self-targeting without checking the resulting state after it resolves — this generalizes the same caution already noted for Cyclops's debuff-driven intent recalculation, but specifically warns that the debuff's *target* (self vs. player) is not implied by the intent label alone. Medium-high confidence (2 reproductions, 2 different enemies, same run).

**"First hit per turn grants Block" enemy mechanics (e.g., Phantasmal Gardener's Skittish) diminish multi-hit-same-target value but do not stop status effects from landing:** Against enemies whose Block triggers on their first hit taken each round and resets every round, a second/third hit on the *same* target within the same turn has its damage mostly or fully absorbed by that fresh Block — making single-hit-per-enemy spreading more damage-efficient than concentrating multiple cards on one target. However, confirmed once (`playtests/runs/silent-2026-08-31.md` E08-A1) that a card's non-damage effect (Weak from Neutralize) still applies even when its damage portion is fully absorbed by the freshly-triggered Block. Spreading hits alone does not solve the deeper problem of reducing the *number* of simultaneously-attacking enemies each round in a multi-enemy fight — that requires AoE or precise exact-lethal single-target kills. Low-medium confidence (single run; Weak-lands-through-Block sub-finding observed only once).



No strategy is assumed. Begin with the character's stated mechanics and starting deck, then test card floor, synergy ceiling, consistency, resource economy, and upgrade value. Promote conclusions here only after they survive the confounder review in the corresponding run reports.
