## Run Assessment

### Character Thesis

Cyclops (this build, mid-run adoption at Act 1 Floor 2) plays as a Breach-centric damage engine anchored by Phoenix Force Visor: Strikes and dedicated Breach attacks (Optic Breach+, Find the Opening+) bypass enemy Block entirely and trigger a Vulnerable+temp-Strength chain on nearly every attack, letting the deck largely ignore enemy Block-stacking patterns (confirmed cleanly against Nibbit E03, Cubex Construct E11). Defense is Block-card-dependent (Defend, Equilibrium, Wide Angle Blast+'s attached Block, later We've Trained for This/Trust Yourself) rather than dodge- or Thorns-based, which worked well against fully-telegraphed single/double hits (Bygone Effigy E13, most of Vantom E18) but has two now-confirmed failure modes: (a) a fresh-shuffle opening hand can occasionally draw zero Block cards against an already-attacking enemy (Bowlbug R1, 22 unavoidable dmg), and (b) at least one enemy type (Hunter Killer) directly punishes the deck's core "stack several cheap Block cards in one turn" pattern via a Dexterity-decay Debuff (Tender), which was the proximate mechanism of this run's death. The deck also accumulated 3 simultaneous Powers (Definitely the Good Guy, Break Their Guard, Good Guy Protocol) without ever isolating any single one's payoff in a clean controlled test, leaving their true value still largely unconfirmed at run end.

### Strongest Evidence

- Observation: E18a (Vantom fight) — Breach-tagged attacks (Strike+, Find the Opening+) triggered Phoenix Force Visor's Vulnerable/temp-Strength grant and Break Their Guard's energy-refund/draw on every play against a 0-Block enemy, while non-Breach attacks (Wide Angle Blast+, Concussive Beam+) played against the identical enemy state in adjacent rounds triggered neither, reproduced across 8+ paired instances within one fight.
  - Interpretation: The Breach/Phoenix-Force-Visor/Break-Their-Guard engine is a precise, reliably-triggering mechanic (Breach tag specifically, independent of whether Block was actually present to break) rather than a generic "any attack" bonus — high confidence given the reproduction count.
- Observation: E23 (Bowlbug fight) — Bowlbug (Rock)'s telegraphed attack was fully absorbed by Block equal to or exceeding its value in 2 separate instances (R2->R3, R4->R5), and its intent flipped live from "Attack N" to "Stunned: can't act on its next turn" both times, confirmed via direct before/after state reads.
  - Interpretation: The "Imbalanced" enemy Debuff (full-block triggers a Stun) is a real, reproducible stun-lock mechanic that a Block-focused deck can exploit for free damage turns — high confidence given 2 clean reproductions in a single fight.
- Observation: E26/E26a (Hunter Killer, fatal fight) — a new "Tender" enemy Debuff (lose 1 Str/Dex per card played this turn) was applied once, after which 2 separate Block-stacking turns (round 2's 4-card sequence, round 4's fatal turn) showed declining per-card Block values as more cards were played in the same turn, and the fight ended in death when a Block-less hand met a lethal attack with no in-hand mitigation.
  - Interpretation: At least one enemy type directly and effectively counters this deck's core Block-stacking defensive pattern; this is a single data point (one enemy, one fight) and should not yet be generalized to "Cyclops's Block strategy is broadly countered," but it is the clearest and most consequential adverse interaction observed this run — medium confidence pending a second Tender-applying encounter.

### Confounders

- Agent misplays: One recorded — E26/round 4 of the fatal Hunter Killer fight, an index-shift misplay caused Definitely the Good Guy (a non-Block, non-mitigating Power) to be played instead of the intended Strike+, burning 2 energy on a card with no same-turn defensive value during the round that ended in death. This did not change the fatal outcome (no card in the hand could have prevented the lethal 12-dmg hit at 6 HP regardless of how the 2 energy was spent), so it is not attributed as the cause of death, but is recorded as a process error for completeness.
- Game variance: The fatal fight's round-4 opening hand (8 cards, zero Block-capable) coincided with the enemy's Tender Debuff already having decayed Dexterity from 2 prior Block-stacking turns; whether an equivalent hand without Tender active would have contained a Block card cannot be determined from a single instance.
- Relic or encounter dependence: Phoenix Force Visor and the broader Breach engine were present from mid-run adoption (Floor 2) onward, so no comparison baseline without it exists this run (see Balance Flags table). Molten Egg's shop-preview upgrade behavior (confirmed only against already-in-shop cards, not an actual new pickup) is a partially-untested mechanic question left open by the run's early end.
- Tooling limitations: Session started mid-combat at Floor 2 (Neow choice, floor 1 encounter, and any pre-adoption rewards are unobserved). One `edit` tool path-parameter error and one `old_str` mismatch (both from table-cell formatting assumptions) were encountered and corrected during report-writing in a prior segment; no state-reading tooling failures occurred during the final combat.
- Suspected bugs: None observed. All new mechanics this run (Imbalanced/Stun, Tender, Flutter/Stun-counter, Swipe, Slippery, Shrink) resolved consistently with their displayed card/status text across every reproduction.

## Guide Promotions

The following findings were reproduced multiple times within this run and are recommended for promotion to `GUIDE.md`'s Cyclops section:

1. **Powers reset between combats (not persistent).** Confirmed directly in E23 (Bowlbug fight): Good Guy Protocol, Break Their Guard, and Definitely the Good Guy all had to be re-played from the hand at the start of a new combat, observed as ordinary unplayed cards in the draw pile rather than already-active statuses carried over from the prior fight. High confidence (direct before/after state observation, unambiguous).
2. **Breach-tagged attacks (not "any Block-break") trigger Phoenix Force Visor and Break Their Guard.** Refined from an earlier broader assumption via E18a: only cards with the Breach tag (Strike/Strike+ via the relic, Optic Breach+, Find the Opening+) trigger the Vulnerable/temp-Strength grant and the energy-refund/draw, independent of whether the target enemy had any Block to break; non-Breach attacks (Wide Angle Blast+, Concussive Beam+) never trigger either bonus even against a 0-Block enemy. Reproduced 8+ times in one fight. High confidence.
3. **Enemy "Imbalanced" Debuff — full-block triggers a Stun.** An enemy with this Debuff becomes Stunned (skips its next turn) if its telegraphed attack is fully blocked (Block >= the attack's damage at the moment it resolves), confirmed reproducible twice in E23 (Bowlbug/Rock). This creates an exploitable stun-lock loop for Block-capable decks. Medium-high confidence (2 clean reproductions, single enemy type).
4. **Enemy "Tender" Debuff — a hard counter to Block-stacking turns.** An enemy with this Debuff causes the player to lose 1 Strength and 1 Dexterity (this turn only) each time a card is played, directly decaying the value of every subsequent card played in the same turn — a direct counter to decks (like this Cyclops build) that rely on stacking several cheap Block/Skill cards per turn rather than a few high-value ones. Observed once this run (Hunter Killer, Floor 22) but reproduced consistently across 2 separate turns within that single fight. Medium confidence pending a second enemy/encounter confirming this is not encounter-specific.
5. **Debuff-driven intent changes recalculate live, not just at resolution.** Confirmed across 3 separate instances this run (E18c's Strength-crash on Vantom, and this run's Weak applications in E26/round 3 and round 4 on Hunter Killer): applying a damage-reducing debuff (Weak, or a Strength-loss effect) immediately updates the enemy's displayed attack-intent value in the same state read, before the attack actually resolves. High confidence (3 reproductions across 2 different debuff types and 2 different enemies).
