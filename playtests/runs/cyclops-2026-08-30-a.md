## Run Assessment

### Character Thesis

Cyclops's game plan observed this run centers on a Breach-triggered "break engine": Breach-keyword attacks (Optic Breach+, Find the Opening, Ricochet Beam) always count as breaking enemy Block regardless of the enemy's current Block value (confirmed repeatedly, consistent with GUIDE.md), which fires Cyclops Battle Visor (Vulnerable + temp Strength) and optionally Break Their Guard (Energy + draw) and Crippling Breach (Frail) on every trigger. This produces strong single-target and, via Ricochet Beam, even stronger multi-hit burst (up to 4 procs from one 2-energy card). The character's resource economy is otherwise conventional Ironclad-style Strength/Block, with temporary Strength/Dexterity from Cyclone Kick/Field Training providing modest short-term scaling. The defensive model is Block-based with no persistent mitigation (no Thorns/passive damage reduction observed), meaning survivability depends entirely on drawing enough Block cards in the same turn a big attack lands — this run's death occurred precisely when that dependency failed twice in the same boss fight (E23, E24) against a 3-enemy encounter whose combined threat output was actively climbing every round via enemy self-buffs. The deck built this run leaned offense-heavy (many single-copy Attacks: Point-Blank Shot, Give Them the Forecast, Wide Beam, Wide Angle Blast, Ricochet Beam, Optic Breach+, Find the Opening, Cyclone Kick) relative to its defensive card count (4 base Defends + Calculated Angle, i.e., only 5 Block sources in a ~22-card deck), which is consistent with — and possibly a contributing cause of — the fatal Block-drought hands in the final fight.

### Strongest Evidence

- Observation: In E19 and E21, a single Ricochet Beam play (2 energy) distributed its 4 Breach hits across live enemies and independently triggered Cyclops Battle Visor on each hit — including landing 2 hits on the same target in a 3-enemy fight (E19) and all 4 hits on the sole target in a 1-enemy fight (E21) — each time producing multiple stacked Vulnerable/temp-Strength applications from one card play.
  - Interpretation: Ricochet Beam is a strong, condition-independent break-engine amplifier: its per-hit (not per-card or per-unique-target) proc behavior means it scales in value with more live enemies (more independent break events) while never underperforming even against a single enemy (all hits concentrate for guaranteed quadruple-proc burst). This makes it a high-floor, high-ceiling pick for any Battle-Visor-style deck.
- Observation: In the Act 1 Boss fight (floor 17), the player's hand contained zero Block-type cards in Round 3 (19 incoming, HP 20→3 after the forced all-in turn) and only one Defend in Round 5 (~27 incoming, HP 3→0, run-ending).
  - Interpretation: A deck with only 5 Block sources in ~22 cards produced two critically low-Block hands in the same high-pressure fight, and the second one was fatal. This suggests the deck's offense/defense ratio, chosen incrementally through this run's reward decisions (multiple Attack picks: Point-Blank Shot, Wide Beam, Ricochet Beam vs. only one defensive-leaning pick, Calculated Angle, plus the Vambrace relic), left insufficient defensive card density to reliably survive a multi-enemy Boss encounter whose combined threat output was climbing every round.

### Confounders

- Agent misplays: In E21 (Vine Shambler, floor 15), deliberately skipped all Block in Round 1 to maximize damage output, taking a full unblocked 12-dmg hit that dropped HP from 30→18 (43%→26%) — a risk that wasn't strictly necessary since the fight wasn't otherwise time-critical, and established a risk-tolerant pattern later applied (unavoidably) in the fatal Boss fight. In the Boss fight's Round 1 (E-unlabeled), a card-index shift after playing Defend caused Field Training to be played instead of the intended 2nd Strike — a minor tactical deviation with negligible HP impact but consistent with the previously-documented index-shift tooling risk.
- Game variance: The Act 1 Boss's card-draw RNG produced a zero-Block hand in Round 3 (E23) and a near-zero-Block hand in Round 5 (E24) at the two moments of highest incoming damage; a different draw order could plausibly have produced a survivable outcome with the same deck.
- Relic or encounter dependence: This run's specific Act 1 Boss (Kin Priest + 2x Kin Follower) is a 3-enemy, compounding-Strength-ramp encounter type not seen earlier in the run; results may not generalize to other Act 1 Boss variants. Parrying Shield's "≥10 Block at end of turn deals 6 to a random enemy" and Vambrace's "first Block of combat is doubled" were both directly observed triggering in the Boss fight (Parrying Shield fired in Round 4, dropping Follower0 48→42) — relic-dependent value that would differ in a run without these relics.
- Tooling limitations: No run_id/seed/build version strings exposed via `get_game_state`/`get_compendium` this session (recorded per E00); Ascension defaulted to 1 instead of the requested 0 because no ascension selector was exposed at character-select (E00); large JSON state payloads repeatedly exceeded the inline tool-output size limit during the Boss fight and had to be read via saved temp files with `view_range` — this worked reliably but added several extra tool calls per combat turn.
- Suspected bugs: None confirmed this run. E22 (Minion-flee mechanic) was not directly tested since the Kin Priest never actually died in this fight — flagged as an open verification gap, not a bug.
