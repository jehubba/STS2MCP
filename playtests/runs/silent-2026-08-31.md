## Run Assessment

### Character Thesis

The Silent, at least in this base-game (non-Cyclops) run's observed build, plays as a resource-efficient attrition character rather than a burst or scaling character. Its core resource economy is energy-per-turn (standard 3, occasionally boosted by Happy Flower or an Energy Potion), spent on a mix of cheap/free utility (0-cost Neutralize, 0-cost Shiv via Infinite Blades+) and single-target Attacks with secondary effects (Weak from Sucker Punch+/Neutralize, Poison from Poisoned Stab+). Defense is entirely reactive (Defend/Survivor block cards drawn from the deck, no proactive Dexterity/permanent-block relic observed), meaning defensive consistency is a function of deck density and draw luck rather than a repeatable engine. Innate Powers (Infinite Blades+) provide a small, completely reliable per-turn damage floor without the "setup tempo tax" that non-Innate Powers would incur, since Innate cards guarantee same-turn availability every combat. The character's biggest observed weakness this run was the total absence of AoE/attacker-count-reduction tools until very late (floor 8), leaving it vulnerable to multi-enemy encounters where simultaneous incoming attacks scale faster than a single-target-focused kill rate can remove them.

### Strongest Evidence

- Observation: In the Act 1 Elite (E08, 4x Phantasmal Gardener), HP fell from 60/70 to 10/70 (a 50-HP, 71% loss) over 7 rounds against 4 simultaneously-attacking, self-Strength-buffing enemies, despite consistently prioritizing exact-lethal single-target kills and Skittish-aware single-hit spreading.
  - Interpretation: A no-AoE Silent deck at this power level cannot reduce the number of simultaneous attackers fast enough against a 4-enemy Elite archetype; the fight was only survivable via an Energy Potion bailout on the exact turn it mattered.
- Observation: Immediately following that Elite, the very next map node was a Monster encounter with no Rest Site in between (E10), and the run's player entered at 10/70 HP with 0 potions, ultimately dying on Round 4 when the drawn hand contained zero Block cards against a lethal-magnitude attack.
  - Interpretation: The compounding of a near-death Elite with an unavoidable follow-up combat and no recovery option is a severe risk multiplier; whether this is map-generation variance or a systemic pacing issue (no Rest Site option directly after Act 1's Elite) is unresolved from a single run, but it directly caused the run's death.

### Confounders

- Agent misplays: None identified as directly causal to the death; Round 3 of E10 mixed offense with partial block (5 Block vs partial incoming) rather than full block, which in hindsight may have shaved 1-2 HP of buffer, but the fatal Round 4 draw (zero Block cards) would likely have been fatal regardless given the deck's Defend density (5 of ~20 cards) and draw-pile-reshuffle timing.
- Game variance: The fatal hand (0 Block cards out of 5 drawn) at the exact moment a lethal-magnitude attack landed is a direct draw-variance outcome. The Act 1 Elite's near-death (E08) also involved substantial round-to-round variance in which enemies' Empower/Attack intents aligned simultaneously.
- Relic or encounter dependence: The map's post-Elite node was a Monster encounter with no Rest Site available, directly compounding two hard fights back-to-back with no recovery window. Ring of the Snake and Silver Crucible were both consumed/exhausted by this point in the run and contributed no further value to the final two fights.
- Tooling limitations: Large JSON combat states during the 4-enemy Elite (E08) exceeded the inline 20KB limit and required saving to temp files with targeted `view_range` reads; this did not block correct play but added overhead to state verification. Card play/draw counts in the Card Play Counts table are reconstructed from discard/exhaust pile deltas across snapshots rather than a live per-card counter, so are approximate.
- Suspected bugs: None confirmed. The Weak-applies-despite-absorbed-damage interaction (E08-A1) is flagged as uncertain/worth-reproducing rather than a confirmed bug, since it is plausible intended design.

## Guide Promotions

The following observations are promoted to `GUIDE.md` as they represent mechanics likely to recur across future Silent (and possibly cross-character) runs and were confirmed multiple times or had clear, unambiguous state evidence:

- Innate Powers (e.g., Infinite Blades+) mitigate the "setup Power tempo" risk documented for Cyclops, since they guarantee same-turn availability every combat with no ongoing card-slot cost — this is a durable, cross-character clarification of when the Cyclops-derived Power-tempo caution does and does not apply.
- Enemy "Debuff" intents are not reliably self-directed — confirmed twice this run (E05's Sludge Spinner, E10's Gremlin Merc) that a "Strategic (Debuff)" or similar intent can apply Weak to the PLAYER rather than to the enemy itself. Agents should not assume debuff intents are self-targeting without checking the resulting state.
- Skittish-style "first hit per turn grants Block" mechanics mean multi-hit combos into the same target within one turn strongly diminish in value after the first hit; spreading single hits across multiple such enemies is more damage-efficient per hit, but does not by itself solve the problem of reducing the number of simultaneously-attacking enemies each round — that requires AoE or exact-lethal single-target kills.
